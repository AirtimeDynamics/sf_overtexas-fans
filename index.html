Season schedule

Opening hours.

The 2026 season runs from May 9 to November 1. Hours change by date, so the official Cedar Point calendar is the final reference.

Opening day • May 9, 2026

10 AM

Gates opened at 10 a.m. for the park’s 157th season. Summer closing times vary by date.

HalloWeekends • Fri & Sat

11 AM – 12 AM

Late-October Fridays and Saturdays run from 11 a.m. to midnight, with daytime family fun and nighttime scares.

HalloWeekends • Thu & Sun

Thu 6 PM • Sun 11 AM

Thursday evenings run from 6 p.m. to midnight; Sundays close at 8 p.m. The season ends November 1.

Visit strategy

Plan it right.

One section combining the three practical things visitors usually need: when to come, how to plan a family day, and what to pack.

Best time to visit

Use the official calendar to pick a day, then check again for event-specific hours such as HalloWeekends.

Weekdays in early June or after Labor Day are usually calmer than summer weekends.
Arrive before opening and start at the far end of the park instead of the front.
Boardwalk Nights and HalloWeekends change the rhythm of the day.

Family planning

Start with rides that match your group’s height limits, then work outward area by area instead of crossing the peninsula repeatedly.

Check the height requirement of major rides before queueing.
Camp Snoopy, Planet Snoopy and Kiddy Kingdom are built for younger riders.
Keep meal, rest and shade breaks in the plan instead of treating them as an afterthought.

What to bring

Pack for a long outdoor day on the lakefront, with wind, sun and changing weather.

Comfortable shoes and clothes you can move in all day.
Sun protection, a water bottle and a light layer for evenings by the lake.
Leave loose items with a non-rider or in a locker: some coasters do not allow them in the queue.
How it grew

150+ years of
thrills.

From an 1870 bathing beach to America’s Roller Coast: the milestones that shaped Cedar Point.

1870
1870Beginnings

A Lake Erie bathing resort

Cedar Point opens on its Lake Erie peninsula as a bathing beach and beer garden.

1892
1892First coaster

Switchback Railway

The Switchback Railway becomes the park’s first roller coaster.

1964
1964Classic wood

Blue Streak

Blue Streak opens; it still runs today as the park’s oldest roller coaster.

1976
1976Three inversions

Corkscrew

Corkscrew becomes the world’s first coaster with three inversions, looping over the midway. It celebrated its 50th season in 2026.

1989
1989Hypercoaster

Magnum XL-200

Magnum XL-200 becomes the first complete-circuit coaster taller than 200 ft: the original hypercoaster.

2000
2000Giga coaster

Millennium Force

Millennium Force opens as the first complete-circuit coaster to top 300 ft.

2003
2003Strata coaster

Top Thrill Dragster

Top Thrill Dragster debuts as the first strata coaster, the world’s tallest and fastest at the time.

2013
2013New front gate

GateKeeper

GateKeeper opens with a new main entrance, flying through two keyholes above the gate.

2016
2016Dive coaster

Valravn

Valravn opens as the tallest, fastest and longest dive coaster of its time.

2018
2018Hyper-hybrid

Steel Vengeance

RMC transforms the wooden Mean Streak (1991–2016) into Steel Vengeance, the world’s first hyper-hybrid coaster.

2023
2023Boardwalk

A new lakeside Boardwalk

The Boardwalk replaces the Lakeside Midway and brings the Zamperla Wild Mouse.

2024
2024Rebuilt

Top Thrill 2 & Six Flags

Top Thrill 2 debuts with an LSM triple launch but closes for repairs after its first week; on July 1, Cedar Fair merges with Six Flags.

2025
2025Tilt coaster

Siren’s Curse

Siren’s Curse opens on June 28 as North America’s tallest, fastest and longest tilt coaster, and Top Thrill 2 returns.
